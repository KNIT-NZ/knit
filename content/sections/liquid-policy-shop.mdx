Meaningful participation in policy currently ends with a submission, a template-driven email, or ten minutes before a sleepy Select Committee. Even if you get in the door to have your views heard, what quickly becomes obvious is the ceiling of the format itself: you may object to a clause, but you cannot propose a better one; you may be an expert, but your expertise is summarised for a report and handed to a clerk; nothing about your ideas is tested against what else in the statute book would be impacted.

Our Liquid Policy Shop will be built for that ceiling. It is a public platform for the collaborative development, refinement, and critique of law and policy, where experts, community members, and institutions asynchronously re-draft the actual clauses of legislation, with every contribution traced to its author, checked against the law's real dependencies, and open to public scrutiny.

Users author proposals; the platform shows their working, maps their consequences, and surfaces where they contradict. The two-transparencies distinction from the first platform holds with more force here: the machinery is radically open, but the content a proposal touches is governed. A platform whose whole purpose is to let the public re-draft operative law cannot rely on restraint alone, because its core action is generative. The obligations of Te Tiriti and the tikanga that pre-date the colonial state are structural to it.

This platform makes standing maximally visible. When a professional association, an agency, or a national institution shows up, their track record and who stands behind them can form the reputation of that contribution. People may delegate their voice, revocably and visibly, to those they trust to represent them on a question.

What the Liquid Policy Shop produces are unbelievably powerful suggestions: dependency-checked, provenance-bearing, openly supported. Their force comes precisely from arming the decision rather than pre-empting it.

Even without formal political adoption, these outputs provide a high-fidelity signal of considered public reasoning and a concrete repository of reform logic. The Liquid Policy Shop lets us prototype the law in public, then feed its drafts, annotated amendments, and structured critiques into formal channels, or inspire new ones. It remains held by the charitable trust on the same terms as everything else the trust builds, including the willingness to pass it on once its users decide where an instrument this potent should ultimately live.

For governments, this is a step change in how they receive advice and invite deliberative perspective. For the public, it is the digital millennium's answer to the black box of policymaking that has made politics divisive and derided.
